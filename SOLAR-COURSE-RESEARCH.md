## Pass 1 — Design + Full Bill of Materials (5kW / 12kWh / 48V)

**Synthesis:** For a ~5kW solar / 12kWh LiFePO4 / 48V homestead off-grid build, the verified consensus is: choose 48V as the default battery voltage (it is universally recommended above ~2kW of PV because raising voltage proportionally cuts current — 4000W draws ~333A at 12V but only ~83A at 48V — which slashes I²R cable losses and lets you use far smaller, cheaper wire and lower-rated overcurrent protection). Use a DC-coupled architecture, ideally built around a single all-in-one inverter/charger (the EG4 6000XP — 6kW 120/240V inverter, 8kW PV MPPT input — is the representative mid-size unit), because DC-coupling is simpler, more robust, and more efficient (~99% MPPT charging vs ~88-92% AC-coupled) and is the right topology for everything under ~6-8kW; AC-coupling only becomes advantageous above that. Size the inverter to at least 1.2x continuous load and remember ratings derate with heat (a 6kW unit at 25C may deliver only ~4.8kW at 40C); for a ~5kW/5500W 48V inverter, established rule-of-thumb tables call for 4/0 AWG battery cable with a ~250-400A breaker/fuse, and an MPPT controller passes proportionally more PV at higher voltage (a 100A controller ≈ 4,800W at 48V). The component-by-component BOM (panels, racking, MPPT/all-in-one, LiFePO4 bank + BMS, inverter, combiner box, DC/AC disconnects, breakers/fuses, SPD, busbars, shunt, cable, grounding) hinges on safety fundamentals: exactly ONE neutral-ground bond (enabled for off-grid, disabled when permanently grid-tied), required manual DC and AC disconnects, and proper EGC bonding — with the firm caveat that NEC and local AHJ rules vary and must be verified against the builder's own gear manuals and jurisdiction.

**⚠️ Caveats:** Source quality is mixed: the strongest claims rest on Victron (manufacturer/primary), Clean Energy Reviews, AltE Store, GreenLancer (reputable installer/vendor secondary sources), and HeatSpring (NEC training). Several BOM and sizing claims rest on single vendor blogs (Anern, mobile-solarpower.com) — adequate for non-extraordinary rules of thumb that are corroborated elsewhere, but not authoritative code. All wire-gauge, fuse, and overcurrent figures are RULES OF THUMB that vary by cable run length, ambient temperature, and the specific equipment datasheet — the builder MUST verify against their own gear's manuals. CODE IS JURISDICTION-DEPENDENT: NEC editions differ and the local Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) is final; every grounding/bonding/disconnect statement here is awareness-level only and must be confirmed locally, ideally with a licensed electrician for AC tie-in and inspection-bound work. Several intermediate sizing refinements were REFUTED in verification and deliberately omitted: the specific MPPT-controller-sizing formula using charging voltage (57.6V) and the 1.25 factor, the cold-temperature Voc×1.2 max-input-voltage rule, the Imp×strings×1.25 array-current method, and the claim that off-grid systems always need a building permit — none survived adversarial voting, so do not present those as settled. Temperature derating magnitude for inverters is model-specific (the 6kW→4.8kW example is illustrative, not universal). Time-sensitivity: the EG4 6000XP specs are current as of mid-2026 but product lineups and prices move; re-verify the chosen unit's datasheet at build time.

### Verified findings (11)

**1. [high · 3-0 (merged from 4 claims)]** 48V is the correct default battery voltage for a ~5kW homestead build because raising system voltage proportionally cuts current and therefore cable losses; 48V is recommended for any battery-based off-grid system above ~2kW of PV, and 12V is not recommended above 2000W of AC draw.
   - *Evidence:* Basic physics (I = P/V): 4000W draws ~333A at 12V, ~166A at 24V, ~83A at 48V. Lower current means lower resistance/I²R cable losses, smaller cheaper wire, and lower-rated overcurrent protection for the same power. GreenLancer states 48V is 'the right default' above 2kW of PV; Clean Energy Reviews states 12V is not recommended above 2000W AC. This is the central architecture decision for the 5kW/48V target system and is essentially unanimous across Victron, EG4, Battle Born, and DIY references.
   - *Sources:* https://www.cleanenergyreviews.info/blog/designing-off-grid-hybrid-solar-systems, https://www.greenlancer.com/post/mppt-solar-charge-controllers

**2. [high · 3-0 (merged from 3 claims)]** Use a DC-coupled architecture for a ~5kW build: it is simpler, more robust, requires no programming, and charges batteries more efficiently (~99% via MPPT vs ~88-92% AC-coupled). DC-coupling suits small mobile (RV) and small/mid residential off-grid systems; AC-coupling only becomes advantageous above roughly 6-8kW.
   - *Evidence:* Victron: 'The big advantage of the DC solar system is its simplicity. It is a very robust design that allows a very reliable and efficient charging of batteries.' DC charges the battery directly via MPPT, avoiding the extra DC-AC-DC conversion that AC-coupling incurs (Clean Energy Reviews: up to 99% MPPT charging vs 88-92% AC). A ~5kW array (~12-13 panels) sits squarely in DC's sweet spot; AC-coupling pays off only at larger scale where running low-voltage DC back to the inverter becomes costly. Corroborated by EnergySage, A1SolarStore, Morningstar.
   - *Sources:* https://www.victronenergy.com/live/drafts:off-grid_documents:choosing_dc_and_or_ac_solar, https://www.cleanenergyreviews.info/blog/ac-coupling-vs-dc-coupling-solar-battery-storage

**3. [high · 3-0 (merged from 2 claims)]** A ~5kW-class 48V system can be built around a single all-in-one inverter/charger that integrates inverter, MPPT solar charge controller, and AC battery charger; the EG4 6000XP (6,000W 120/240V split-phase inverter, 8,000W PV input) is the representative mid-size unit. All-in-one (hybrid) inverters combine these three functions in one box.
   - *Evidence:* EG4's own product page and spec sheet confirm 6,000W continuous AC, 120/240V split-phase, 8,000W PV via dual MPPTs, 48V, all-in-one. mobile-solarpower.com lists it as the 'Medium' all-in-one choice. Hybrid/all-in-one units integrate MPPT charge controller + pure-sine inverter + AC charger (corroborated by PowMr, LVYUAN, SankoPower). This simplifies the BOM and wiring versus separate components, which is preferred for a DIY ~5kW build.
   - *Sources:* https://www.mobile-solarpower.com/48v-complete-system-blueprint-559371.html, https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/off-grid-solar-solutions/off-grid-solar-bill-materials-include

**4. [high · 3-0 (merged from 2 claims)]** Inverter sizing: rate continuous output at least 1.2x maximum continuous load, derate for heat (a 6kW unit rated at 25C may deliver only ~4.8kW at 40C), and pick ~5kW for an average home or 8kW+ for larger homes with high-power appliances.
   - *Evidence:* Clean Energy Reviews: 'the inverter should be at least 1.2 times larger than the maximum continuous summer demand' and 'a 6kW inverter rated at 25C may only output 4.8kW at 40C.' Anern: '5 kW inverter handles average off-grid home peak demand; larger homes with high-power appliances need 8 kW or more.' Both the 1.2x margin and temperature derating are standard, datasheet-dependent engineering rules of thumb; exact derating magnitude varies by model and must be checked against the unit's own datasheet.
   - *Sources:* https://www.cleanenergyreviews.info/blog/designing-off-grid-hybrid-solar-systems, https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/off-grid-solar-solutions/off-grid-solar-bill-materials-include

**5. [high · 3-0 (merged from 2 claims)]** Battery cable and overcurrent sizing scale with both inverter wattage and system voltage. For a 48V system, AltE recommends 2/0 AWG with a 110A fuse/breaker for a 3000W inverter, and 4/0 AWG with a 400A fuse (or 250A breaker) for 5500W — directly applicable to a ~5kW 48V build. The same power at higher voltage needs far smaller cable (3000W needs 4/0 AWG at 12V but only 2/0 AWG at 48V).
   - *Evidence:* AltE Store table (verified verbatim): 48V 3000W = 2/0 AWG, 110A; 48V 5500W = 4/0 AWG, 400A fuse/250A breaker; 12V 3000W = 4/0 AWG, 400A/250A. A 5kW/48V inverter draws ~104-115A continuous; fuses are sized 125-175% of load with surge/voltage-drop headroom, which is why the 400A figure far exceeds steady draw. Method corroborated by DIY Solar Forum, RenewableWise, theinverterstore. Exact gauge varies with run length and brand — verify per install.
   - *Sources:* https://www.altestore.com/pages/recommended-inverter-cable-breaker-fuse-sizing

**6. [high · 3-0]** An MPPT charge controller is rated by its output (battery-side) current, so it passes proportionally more PV wattage at higher battery voltage: a 100A controller handles roughly 1,200W at 12V, 2,400W at 24V, and 4,800W at 48V. Max PV watts ≈ output amps × battery voltage.
   - *Evidence:* GreenLancer: '100A controller handles roughly 1,200W on 12V, 2,400W on 24V, 4,800W on 48V.' Corroborated by Renogy, EPEVER (1250/2500/5000W), and LiTime datasheets, all showing proportional doubling per voltage step. Exact PV-input caps vary by brand because the precise multiplier uses charging voltage (~57.6V for 48V LiFePO4), so 'roughly' is the right framing. For a ~5kW array at 48V, plan for ~100A+ of MPPT capacity (e.g., the EG4 6000XP's 8kW PV input covers this in one unit).
   - *Sources:* https://www.greenlancer.com/post/mppt-solar-charge-controllers

**7. [high · 3-0]** LiFePO4 is the preferred off-grid battery chemistry (4,000-8,000+ cycle life). Typical off-grid homes use 10-30 kWh of capacity; a 10 kWh bank provides roughly 1-2 days of autonomy depending on consumption, so the 12 kWh target is a reasonable starting bank.
   - *Evidence:* Anern: 'Typical off-grid home uses 10-30 kWh capacity; a 10 kWh bank provides 1-2 days autonomy depending on consumption.' Corroborated by Renogy, EPEVER, EvLithium on LiFePO4 cycle life (3,000-6,000+ consensus, premium cells higher). A 12 kWh LiFePO4 bank at ~80-90% DoD yields ~10 kWh usable — about a day at typical ~8-10 kWh/day loads, more for frugal users. Autonomy is load-dependent.
   - *Sources:* https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/off-grid-solar-solutions/off-grid-solar-bill-materials-include

**8. [medium · 3-0]** A PV combiner box consolidates multiple panel strings into one feed and houses per-string fuses/breakers, busbars, an optional disconnect, and an optional surge protection device (SPD). It is needed when you have multiple strings to aggregate and protect.
   - *Evidence:* Anern: 'A PV combiner box takes the output from several strings of solar panels and combines them into one main feed.' Internal components (per-string PV fuses, positive/negative busbars, DC SPD, DC isolator/disconnect, PE bar) corroborated by viox.com and others; disconnect and SPD are correctly flagged as optional-but-recommended. Note: the related claim that a combiner is 'only necessary above three strings' was REFUTED 0-3 — do not state a hard three-string threshold.
   - *Sources:* https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/diy-solar-guides/combiner-boxes-isolators-disconnects

**9. [high · 3-0]** Manually operated disconnect switches that fully de-energize a circuit for service are required by NEC for most PV systems, on BOTH the DC (array-to-inverter) and AC (inverter-to-panel) sides.
   - *Evidence:* Anern: 'A disconnect switch is a manually operated switch designed to completely de-energize a circuit for service or maintenance.' NEC 690.13 mandates a main PV disconnect and 690.15 mandates DC and AC disconnecting means for inverters/charge controllers (corroborated by ExpertCE, EC&M). Caveat: line-side terminals can remain energized when open (NEC requires a warning label), so 'fully de-energizes' is a slight simplification of the downstream-only effect.
   - *Sources:* https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/diy-solar-guides/combiner-boxes-isolators-disconnects

**10. [high · 3-0 (merged from 2 claims)]** Grounding/bonding fundamentals: neutral and equipment grounding conductor (EGC) must be bonded at exactly ONE point via a main bonding jumper; multiple bonds put objectionable return current on grounding conductors. For off-grid the inverter's ground-neutral bond is ENABLED; for a permanently grid-connected system it is DISABLED (the bond lives at the utility service entrance).
   - *Evidence:* HeatSpring: MBJ connects EGC to neutral 'at one point only'; multiple bonds cause 'current flowing back through the grounding conductor.' mobile-solarpower.com: 'Each system can have only ONE ground neutral bond... offgrid, ENABLE the ground neutral bond... grid permanently, DISABLE.' Reflects NEC 250.30 separately-derived-system doctrine, corroborated by DIY Solar Forum and NAWS. Verify exactly where the single bond physically sits in your build.
   - *Sources:* https://blog.heatspring.com/guidelines-for-designing-grounding-systems-for-solar-pv-installations-in-accordance-with-the-nec/, https://www.mobile-solarpower.com/48v-complete-system-blueprint-559371.html

**11. [medium · 2-1]** Modern functionally grounded inverters (transformerless, UL1741-listed, with built-in ground-fault protection) eliminate the need for a SEPARATE DC grounding electrode system by connecting the PV-circuit EGC to the inverter's grounding point — but module-frame EGC bonding is still required.
   - *Evidence:* HeatSpring: functionally grounded inverters 'eliminate the need for a separate DC grounding system' by connecting the PV-circuit EGC to the inverter's grounding point, with the inverter 'listed to UL1741.' Reflects NEC 690.47 (post-2017). What is eliminated is the separate DC grounding ELECTRODE/GEC — NOT the EGC; module frames must still be bonded per 690.43. Split vote (2-1) reflects nuance, not error — verify your specific inverter's listing and manual.
   - *Sources:* https://blog.heatspring.com/guidelines-for-designing-grounding-systems-for-solar-pv-installations-in-accordance-with-the-nec/

## Pass 2 — Reading Spec Sheets + Buying Smart

**Synthesis:** To buy off-grid solar gear smart, a DIYer must read the datasheet, not the product name or hero number: spec sheets routinely advertise one number while the binding constraint is a different one. For panels, the STC nameplate (1,000 W/m2, 25C, AM1.5) is a lab figure — real modules run 20-35C hotter at noon, so harvest is below nameplate; the safety-critical number is open-circuit voltage Voc, which RISES as temperature falls and, uncorrected, can push a cold-morning string above the inverter/controller max DC input voltage and permanently destroy it (multiple primary sources: Victron, EG4, PSU AE868). For inverters, the two traps are continuous-vs-surge watts (e.g. Victron RS sustains ~4800-5300W but peaks 9kW for only 3 seconds; EG4 12000XP sustains 15000W but surges 15360W for ~10s, battery-only) and AC-output-vs-PV-input (EG4 12000XP outputs 12-15kW AC but ingests up to 24kW PV — roughly double; Victron RS outputs 4800-5300W AC but accepts only 4000W DC solar). For batteries, Ah is meaningless without voltage (EG4 LifePower4: 51.2V x 100Ah = 5.12kWh nameplate), and the BMS continuous current limit (100A ~ 5.1kW) can bottleneck a far larger inverter regardless of the inverter's rating. The through-line for buying smart: compare continuous (not peak) AC watts, usable (not nameplate) kWh, PV-input vs AC-output as separate lines, and always temperature-correct Voc against your own unit's datasheet.

**⚠️ Caveats:** Source strength is mixed by topic. The hard equipment-spec claims (PV-input vs AC-output, continuous vs surge, max PV input voltage, battery Ah/voltage/BMS limits) are backed by PRIMARY manufacturer datasheets (EG4 12000XP, EG4 LifePower4 via Signature Solar, Victron Inverter RS and SPM50-12) and are high confidence — but they are SPECIFIC to those units; the buyer must read their own unit's datasheet because numbers vary by brand and even by model revision (the EG4 12000XP continuous rating shifted from 12kW to 15kW between hardware revisions). The temperature/Voc physics claims rest partly on blog sources (anernstore, medium, thegreenwatt, solarbasicshub) — these are individually low-authority, but the underlying physics is settled, code-mandated (NEC 690.7), and was independently corroborated and arithmetically re-verified, so confidence stays high. The PORTABLE POWER STATION / solar-generator category (AllPowers/Jackery/EcoFlow ~2000W class) requested in the question is NOT covered by any surviving claim — the rated-vs-surge trap, Wh-vs-runtime, and solar-input-limit points for that category were not verified here and should be treated as unconfirmed. Eight related claims were refuted in voting, most notably the clean MPPT 'same 100A passes 4x more watts at 48V than 12V' framing and the DC-to-AC ratio definition — so those specific talking points should NOT be used as cited facts even though the underlying principle is real. All datasheet revisions cited are 2024-2025; re-check current sheets before purchase.

### Verified findings (7)

**1. [high · 3-0 (EG4) and 3-0 (Victron)]** PV INPUT rating and AC OUTPUT rating are separate spec lines and a unit's name can match one while it does the other to a very different number — buyers must read them apart. EG4 12000XP: up to 24kW usable PV in (28kW recommended), but only 12kW AC out without PV / 15kW with PV — PV input is roughly double AC output. Victron Inverter RS Smart Solar: 4800-5300W continuous AC out but only 4000W max DC solar charging in.
   - *Evidence:* EG4 12000XP spec sheet (primary, v1.2.6 2025): 'MAX. UTILIZED SOLAR POWER 24000W (12000 per MPPT)', 'MAX. RECOMMENDED SOLAR INPUT 28000W', vs 'NOMINAL POWER OUTPUT w/ PV: 15000W @ 240V; w/out PV: 12000W @ 240V'. 24kW PV / 12kW base AC = exactly 2.0x. Victron RS (primary, datasheet rev 12 06/2025): 'Continuous output power at 25C: Increases linearly from 4800 W at 46 VDC to 5300 W at 52 VDC' vs 'Maximum DC solar charging power: 4000 W' — two distinct lines on the same unit. Excess PV beyond AC output goes to battery charging or is unused.
   - *Sources:* https://eg4electronics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/EG4-12000XP-Spec-Sheet.pdf, https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Inverter_RS_Smart_Solar/en/technical-specifications.html

**2. [high · 3-0 (EG4) and 3-0 (Victron)]** Continuous and peak/surge watts are separately rated; surge is a brief, time-limited number far above continuous and often battery-only — never size a load to the surge figure. EG4 12000XP: 15000W continuous (L1-L2) vs 15360W surge for only ~10s, battery only. Victron RS: ~4800-5300W continuous vs 9kW for 3 seconds and 7kW for 4 minutes.
   - *Evidence:* EG4 12000XP (primary): 'MAX. CONTINUOUS WATTAGE 15000W (L1-L2) | 7500W (L1-N or L2-N)' and 'PEAK POWER (SURGE CAPACITY) 15360W for ~10s' with footnote 'With battery only.' Victron RS (primary): 'Peak power: 9 kW for 3 seconds, 7 kW for 4 minutes' vs continuous 4800-5300W at 25C (derating to 4500W at 40C, 3000W at 65C). Surge is a separate spec row, not a sustained capability. Note: EG4 15kW-continuous figure applies to the updated IV-15000-XP model; earlier 12000XP units cap continuous at 12kW — read your exact unit's sheet.
   - *Sources:* https://eg4electronics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/EG4-12000XP-Spec-Sheet.pdf, https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Inverter_RS_Smart_Solar/en/technical-specifications.html

**3. [high · 3-0 (PSU AE868) plus 3-0 / 3-0 supporting (Victron, EG4)]** Every inverter/controller has a manufacturer-specified PV input voltage RANGE: a hard maximum the array's Voc must never exceed (exceeding it permanently destroys the input stage and voids warranty) and a minimum below which it won't operate. The PV string's open-circuit voltage (Voc), not its max-power voltage (Vmp), is what must be checked against the max because Voc is the highest voltage the open array presents.
   - *Evidence:* PSU AE868 (primary): 'Inverters are designed to operate within a voltage range, which is set by the manufacturer's specification datasheet'; 'PV designers should choose the PV array maximum voltage in order not to exceed the maximum input voltage of the inverter'; 'Each inverter has a minimum input voltage value that cannot trigger the inverter to operate if the PV voltage is lower than what is listed.' EG4 12000XP (primary): 'MAX. PV INPUT VOLTAGE 500 VDC ... Any damage caused by reaching >500 VDC will not be covered under warranty' (design range 100-480 VDC, 500 VDC absolute ceiling). Victron SPM50-12 example (primary): module Voc 22.2V vs Vmpp 18V at STC; Voc is the value used for cold-weather string math. NEC 690.7 requires string Voc be computed at the site record-low temperature. For DC-coupled all-in-ones the relevant max is the internal MPPT's PV voltage limit.
   - *Sources:* https://courses.ems.psu.edu/ae868/node/905, https://eg4electronics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/EG4-12000XP-Spec-Sheet.pdf, https://www.victronenergy.com/blog/2014/03/28/matching-victron-energy-solar-modules-to-the-new-mppt-charge-regulators/

**4. [high · 3-0 (anernstore + medium examples), 2-0 (thegreenwatt coefficient), 3-0 (Victron worked example)]** Voc rises as cell temperature falls, peaking on cold clear mornings, so cold-weather Voc drives the upper limit of string sizing. The temperature coefficient of Voc (beta) for crystalline silicon is typically -0.27 to -0.32%/C (textbook range -0.25 to -0.35%/C). The correct sizing formula is Voc_string_cold = N x Voc_STC x (1 + (beta/100) x (T_min - 25)). Failing to temperature-correct Voc is the most common and most dangerous string-sizing error.
   - *Evidence:* Victron primary worked example: three SPM50-12 modules (22.2V Voc each, -0.34%/C) in series reach 3 x (22.2 + (-0.34% x -35C)) = 74.5V at -10C; 'Exceeding the input voltage range will damage the controller permanently.' Medium guide (corroborated by NEC 690.7, Mayfield, SolarPermitSolutions): formula 'Voc_string_cold = N x Voc_STC x (1 + gamma_Voc / 100 x (T_min - 25))'; worked example '20-module string safe at STC reaches 1077V at -10C, exceeding the 1000V limit by 7.7%.' Anernstore example: 50.0V STC Voc, -15C, -0.28%/C -> 55.6V (~11% rise), arithmetic verified exact. TheGreenWatt: beta 'typically -0.27 to -0.32%/C for crystalline silicon', corroborated by PVEducation, Solar.com, Sinovoltaics. Specific voltage examples depend on the chosen module's coefficient — verify against your own datasheet.
   - *Sources:* https://www.victronenergy.com/blog/2014/03/28/matching-victron-energy-solar-modules-to-the-new-mppt-charge-regulators/, https://medium.com/@amrit.mandal0191/how-to-calculate-solar-pv-string-sizing-temperature-corrected-voc-mppt-range-and-the-four-ab76a2fb021b, https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/diy-solar-guides/read-pv-spec-voc-isc-stc, https://www.thegreenwatt.com/temperature-coefficient-voc/

**5. [high · 3-0 (anernstore), 3-0 (solarbasicshub)]** Solar panel STC ratings (1,000 W/m2 irradiance, 25C cell temperature, AM1.5 spectrum) are a lab figure, not a production promise: real modules run 20-35C hotter than 25C at noon and sunlight is often below 1,000 W/m2, so real harvested output is usually below nameplate. The panel datasheet distinguishes Voc/Isc (open-circuit, used for sizing) from Vmp/Imp (max-power, the operating point).
   - *Evidence:* SolarBasicsHub: 'STC is based on 1,000 W/m2 irradiance and 25C cell temperature... In real life, panels usually operate hotter than that, and sunlight is often below 1,000 W/m2, so real output is usually lower.' Anernstore: 'STC uses 25C cell temperature and 1000 W/m2 irradiance. Roof-mounted modules often run 20-35C hotter than 25C at noon, which cuts voltage and power.' Corroborated by SolarQuotes (panels reach 45-65C in sun, 70C+ in summer), with Pmax temperature coefficient ~-0.3 to -0.5%/C costing ~6-15% from heat alone. NMOT/NOCT-rated power is typically ~75% of STC power; PTC (CEC standard) typically runs 10-15% below STC. STC definition is the universal, unchanged-since-1980s industry standard.
   - *Sources:* https://solarbasicshub.com/solar-panel-spec-sheet-explained/, https://www.anernstore.com/blogs/diy-solar-guides/read-pv-spec-voc-isc-stc

**6. [high · 3-0]** Battery capacity in Ah is meaningless without voltage — multiply by nominal voltage to get usable energy. The EG4 LifePower4 48V is 51.2V nominal x 100Ah = 5.12 kWh nameplate storage. This is nameplate, not usable, energy (usable depends on DoD / BMS cutoff).
   - *Evidence:* EG4 LifePower4 48V spec sheet (Signature Solar, primary): 'Storage Capacity 5.12 kWh ... 51.2V 100Ah 5120Wh ... Voltage 51.2V ... Capacity 100Ah.' Arithmetic exact: 51.2 x 100 = 5120 Wh = 5.12 kWh. Corroborated by EG4 Electronics (manufacturer), Current Connected, Redway Power, ShopSolar. Teaching point: Ah alone cannot give kWh without voltage — a 100Ah 12V battery (1.28kWh) and a 100Ah 48V battery (5.12kWh) share the same Ah but store 4x different energy. Current V2 model is identically spec'd.
   - *Sources:* https://signaturesolar.com/content/documents/EG4/EG4%20LifePower4%2048V%20Spec%20Sheet-2.1.0.pdf

**7. [high · 3-0]** A battery's BMS sets a maximum continuous charge AND discharge current that can bottleneck a much larger inverter regardless of the inverter's own rating. The EG4 LifePower4's BMS caps both at 100A, so a single 51.2V battery can sustain only ~5.1kW continuous in or out — which is why these are paralleled to feed larger inverters.
   - *Evidence:* EG4 LifePower4 48V spec sheet (primary): 'Max Constant Charge: 100A (30-50A recommended)' and 'Max Constant Discharge: 100A.' Math: 51.2V x 100A = 5120W = ~5.1kW. Current Connected independently phrases it as 'roughly 5.1kW of power in or out of a single battery.' Implication: pairing one such battery with a 12-15kW inverter (e.g. EG4 12000XP) starves the inverter at high load — you must parallel batteries (or pick higher-current cells) so total BMS continuous current matches the inverter draw. The '30-50A recommended' note is a charge recommendation, not the discharge limit.
   - *Sources:* https://signaturesolar.com/content/documents/EG4/EG4%20LifePower4%2048V%20Spec%20Sheet-2.1.0.pdf

## Pass 3 — Troubleshooting + Common Fault Codes

**Synthesis:** This reference compiles verified, manufacturer-documented fault behavior and field-diagnostic logic for the off-grid gear a capable DIYer actually owns: EG4 hybrids, Victron MPPTs, and Voltronic-based all-in-ones (MPP Solar / Axpert and rebrands like Voltacon), plus Growatt. The strongest, primary-sourced material is the per-brand fault/alarm tables — Victron Err codes (with Err 11 pointing at loose/undersized DC wiring and Err 67 at lost-BMS comms), the MPP Solar/Voltronic numeric table (01 fan, 02 over-temp, 05 short/over-temp, 07 overload, 61/80 BMS-comms, F72 parallel current-sharing), and EG4's plain-language alarms (Temperature fault, Bat com failure vs Bat Fault, AFCI high = PV arc, Trip by GFCI high = AC leakage). The recurring diagnostic spine is consistent across brands: a blank LCD means no battery/DC input (the display is battery-powered) so check battery voltage and DC switch first; over-temp faults point to blocked airflow or ambient heat; overload faults mean shed load; comms-loss faults mean check the cable pinout and that the correct battery brand is selected on the LCD. For panel-level faults, measure each module's open-circuit voltage in full sun and compare — a reading more than ~10% below nameplate (temperature-corrected) or one module significantly lower than its siblings flags that panel. The clear STOP-and-call-an-electrician signals are arc-fault (AFCI) and ground-fault (GFCI) trips, plus any burning smell or melted lugs/connectors.

**⚠️ Caveats:** Fault codes, alarm wording, and even program/menu numbers vary by brand, model, AND firmware version — the same number can mean different things across units (e.g., on the older MPP Solar PIP-HS manual code 07 is a low-battery condition, but on modern Voltronic-based all-in-ones 07 is overload; MPP "Program 23" vs the manuals' "Program 28" for parallel mode). The owner MUST consult their own unit's manual; this reference is general guidance, not a substitute for manufacturer documentation. Source quality is mixed: Victron, EG4, and MPP Solar fault tables are confirmed against PRIMARY manufacturer manuals (high confidence), but the cross-brand Voltronic/MPP code summaries, the no-display diagnosis, and the Growatt codes rely on secondary vendor docs and a DIY-solar blog (medium/low confidence). The panel within-10%-of-rated rule applies to open-circuit voltage measured in full sun at moderate cell temperature — it omits the Voc temperature coefficient (~-0.3%/degC), so cold panels read high and hot panels read low. A 'WHEN TO STOP / call an electrician' finding (arc fault, ground fault, burning smell, melted lugs) was assembled from the manufacturer fault-code semantics (AFCI/GFCI trips) rather than a single dedicated safety source, so its severity framing is synthesized rather than directly quoted.
